# Phase 37.0 - Wheeler-DeWitt Analysis

## Scope
This document studies the continuum Wheeler-DeWitt limit of the homogeneous sector only. It does not claim that WDW is the fundamental quantization of the Hayward candidate.

## Homogeneous WDW equation
For the reduced scale-factor sector, the constraint is

$$\hat H\Psi(a)=0.$$

In schematic form,

$$\left[-\frac{d^2}{da^2}+U_{eff}(a)\right]\Psi(a)=0,$$

where $U_{eff}$ is fixed by the classical collapse potential plus the effective repulsive term inferred from the Hayward/LQC correspondence.

## Regularity near $a\to0$
The classical Schwarzschild/Oppenheimer-Snyder singular limit would drive unbounded curvature. The prior Hayward-LQC phases instead impose:

$$\rho\le\rho_{crit},\qquad R(0)=16.0,\qquad K(0)=42.67.$$

In a WDW representation this appears as a repulsive potential wall or regular de Sitter core at small $a$. Therefore normalizable or finite-amplitude solutions are possible if boundary conditions are chosen to suppress the singular branch.

## Normal solutions
The WDW continuum theory does not automatically select a unique physical inner product or a unique no-singular boundary condition. For this reason the audit gives WDW partial support only:
- finite effective solutions are allowed,
- a regular $\Psi(a)$ near $a=0$ is compatible with the Hayward core,
- no intrinsic area or volume gap is generated by WDW itself,
- the bounce has to be inserted through the effective potential inherited from LQC/Hayward.

## Behavior at the origin
Near the regular core the effective potential behaves as a de Sitter regulator rather than a singular Schwarzschild potential. The acceptable branch satisfies:

$$|\Psi(0)|<\infty,\qquad \int |\Psi(a)|^2\,d\mu(a)<\infty$$

for the chosen minisuperspace measure.

WDW can represent the regular Hayward minisuperspace sector as an effective continuum limit, but it does not explain the minimum length or the density bound. It is therefore compatible as a low-resolution quantization, not as the strongest underlying structure.
