## 2. Information-Theoretic Space and Gravity

We evaluate the primary concepts linking quantum information to geometry:

### 2.1 The Holographic Principle and "It from Qubit"
The Holographic Principle (Bekenstein, 't Hooft, Susskind) establishes that the information contained within a volume of space is encoded on its boundary surface. The maximum information content is bounded by the area of the boundary:
$$I_{\text{max}} = \frac{A}{4 k_B \ln 2 \ l_P^2}$$
This suggests that area is a measure of channel capacity. Spacetime is not a fundamental container, but a holographic reconstruction of boundary quantum states (an "It from Qubit" description).

### 2.2 Quantum Complexity and the Volume Conjecture
Leonard Susskind and collaborators proposed that volume is the geometric dual of **quantum complexity** (the minimal number of quantum gates required to construct a state). This is formulated in two conjectures:
1.  **Complexity = Volume**: The volume $V$ of a bulk Einstein-Rosen bridge is proportional to the quantum complexity $\mathcal{C}$ of the boundary state:
    $$\mathcal{C} \propto \frac{V}{G l_{\text{AdS}}}$$
2.  **Complexity = Action**: The complexity is proportional to the gravitational action $I_{\text{WDW}}$ of the Wheeler-DeWitt patch:
    $$\mathcal{C} = \frac{I_{\text{WDW}}}{\pi \hbar}$$
These conjectures show that the interior expansion of a black hole (and the growth of its Einstein-Rosen bridge) is a representation of the continuous growth of quantum complexity in the boundary state.
