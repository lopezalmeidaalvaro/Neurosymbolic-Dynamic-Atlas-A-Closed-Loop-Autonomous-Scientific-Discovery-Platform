## 2. Classification of Collective RQB Structures

We define four primary classes of collective configurations on the RQB-Event network:

### 2.1 RQB Clusters
- **Definition**: Subgraphs of the RQB network characterized by extremely high mutual information and dense entanglement bonds relative to the rest of the network.
- **Physical Interpretation**: Localized quantum packets. In the infrared limit, clusters act as localized wave packets carrying effective rest mass determined by the internal binding energy of the cluster.

### 2.2 Topological Defects
- **Definition**: Persistent discontinuities or irregularities in the adjacency operator $\hat{A}$ (e.g., localized graph disclinations, twists, or non-trivial cycles) that cannot be removed by local unitary transformations.
- **Physical Interpretation**: Topologically protected states. Because they cannot be resolved locally, these defects are highly stable and behave as particles carrying topological charges.

### 2.3 Persistent Patterns
- **Definition**: Qubit state configurations $|\Psi_{\text{pattern}}\rangle$ that remain invariant or periodic under the action of the pregeometric Liouvillian flow:
  $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{pre}}[\rho_{\text{pattern}}] = 0 \quad (\text{or } i\omega \rho_{\text{pattern}})$$
- **Physical Interpretation**: Stationary states or internal quantum numbers (e.g., charge, flavor).

### 2.4 Propagating Excitations
- **Definition**: Coherent wave-like perturbations of the qubit states or bond configurations that travel across the network via local adjacency links.
- **Physical Interpretation**: Propagating fields. The speed of propagation is bounded by the network's maximum information transport rate (the Lie-Robinson speed limit), which corresponds to the emergent speed of light $c$.
