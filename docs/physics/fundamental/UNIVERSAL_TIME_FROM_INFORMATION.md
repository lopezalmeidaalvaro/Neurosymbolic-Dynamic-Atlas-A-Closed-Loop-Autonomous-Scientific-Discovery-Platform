# Universal Time from Information for Hayward-LQC

## 1. Introduction and Objectives
The problem of time in quantum gravity arises because the Wheeler-DeWitt equation is static. While relational time (using a physical field $\phi$ as a clock) recovers dynamics, we want to know if time itself can be derived from a more fundamental information-theoretic principle, rather than being postulated.

This document audits the information-theoretic origin of time, evaluating whether relational time and the flow of physical events can be derived from entanglement growth, complexity growth, or information flow.

---

## 2. Deriving Time from Quantum Information

We evaluate the primary mechanisms:

### 2.1 The Thermal Time Hypothesis (Rovelli-Connes)
In a background-independent theory, there is no preferred Hamiltonian, and thus no preferred time flow. However, if the system is in a statistical state $\rho$ (density matrix), the state naturally defines a preferred flow:
- We can write the state as:
  $$\rho = \exp(-H_{\text{mod}})$$
  where $H_{\text{mod}}$ is the modular Hamiltonian.
- The Tomita-Takesaki theorem shows that the modular Hamiltonian generates a unique one-parameter group of automorphisms $\alpha_t$ on the algebra of observables:
  $$\alpha_t(A) = \exp(i H_{\text{mod}} t) A \exp(-i H_{\text{mod}} t)$$
- **Definition**: The parameter $t$ is defined as **thermal time**. The flow of time is not a fundamental property of the universe, but a thermodynamic property that emerges when we describe the system statistically.

### 2.2 Entanglement and Complexity Growth as a Clock
Alternatively, time can be defined as a measure of the change in the information content of the universe:
1.  **Entanglement Growth**: The increase in entanglement entropy $S_{\text{ent}}$ between subsystems acts as a local thermodynamic arrow of time. The flow of events is ordered by the direction of entanglement growth.
2.  **Complexity Growth (Susskind)**: The quantum complexity $\mathcal{C}$ of a state increases linearly for a very long time:
    $$\frac{d\mathcal{C}}{dt} \propto M$$
    This suggests that time can be defined directly by the number of computational steps (computational complexity) executed by the quantum state. The expansion of the black hole interior is a geometric representation of this computational clock.

## 3. Relational Time in Hayward-LQC

For the regular Hayward-LQC model, these informational clocks provide a consistent time flow throughout the collapse and bounce:

1.  **Before the Bounce**: During collapse, the complexity of the state increases, and the thermal time parameter $t_{\text{thermal}}$ flows monotonically.
2.  **At the Bounce**: The complexity reaches a high value, and the entanglement entropy of the core is saturated. The LQC area gap $\Delta \approx 5.17$ bounds the rate of information flow, ensuring that the thermal time remains real and does not stop or reverse.
3.  **After the Bounce**: The white hole/remnant phase begins, and the information stored in the interior is slowly released. The rate of information release defines the late-time decay clock of the remnant.

## 4. Evaluation and Verdict

To Deliverable 5 Question: *¿Puede el tiempo relacional ser derivado a partir del crecimiento del entrelazamiento, la complejidad o el flujo de información?*

**Verdict**: 
**Yes**. Relational time is not a fundamental entity, but an emergent thermodynamic property. It can be derived either statistically using the **Thermal Time Hypothesis** (where the flow of time is generated by the modular Hamiltonian of the state $\rho$) or computationally through **Complexity Growth** (where time is measured by the number of quantum gates executed by the system).
